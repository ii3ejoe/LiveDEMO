Our story

About Iowa Neighbor

Iowa Neighbor was created by someone with lived experience of navigating systems that too often hide or withhold practical help. The project exists so that people who are struggling—and the organizations that serve them—can find clear, usable resource information without the usual barriers.

It was built and is maintained on a $0 operating budget, using only free and open tools. The goal is simple: keep community resource information free, open, and available to everyone—from neighbors in need to the nonprofits and partners who support them.

This is a passion project born from a real gap: when information is locked away or hard to reach, people fall through the cracks. Iowa Neighbor is independent, transparent, and focused on access—not gatekeeping.

Availability Before Abundance

Iowa Neighbor was built around a simple idea: useful resources matter most when people can actually access them. We believe availability, quality, and accessibility matter more than the number of resources in a directory.

A smaller set of dependable resources someone can use without the internet can be more valuable than a huge directory they can’t open when connectivity fails.

That’s why offline functionality isn’t an optional extra or a backup plan. It’s a fundamental part of Iowa Neighbor. Online features can expand the collection, provide updates, and connect people with additional services. But the core experience is designed to remain useful without relying on a constant internet connection.

We also recognize that not everyone has the same devices, connectivity, or circumstances. Making resources accessible means considering those realities from the beginning—not treating them as an afterthought.

Iowa Neighbor isn’t trying to collect the most resources or become the biggest directory. Our goal is to make useful, reliable resources available when people need them most.

Availability before abundance. Quality before quantity. Accessibility by design.
